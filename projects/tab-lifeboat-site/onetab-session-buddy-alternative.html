OneTab and Session Buddy: an honest comparison

Both are long-running, free, well-rated tab tools. Here's what each actually does, where it falls short, and where Tab Lifeboat is different. As of September 2026.

Not affiliated with OneTab or Session Buddy; names are used only to identify the products being compared.

1. OneTab, checked directly

Per its Chrome Web Store listing on September 24, 2026: version 2.21, Manifest V3, rated 4.4 out of 5 from about 14,600 ratings, 2,000,000+ users, completely free with no paid tier.

OneTab's job is simple: click it, and every open tab in the window becomes one line on a list page, freeing the memory those tabs used. Click a line to reopen that tab. Its own help page describes drag-and-drop "groups" you build by hand inside that list, and confirms you keep your list across a restart or crash. What it doesn't do, confirmed by SupaSidebar's review: "OneTab does not preserve tab groups, pinned tabs, or window layouts. It flattens everything to a list." There's also no automatic background snapshot — you have to click the icon yourself before OneTab has anything saved. One optional feature, "share as a web page," uploads that tab list to OneTab's own server so you can share a link; OneTab's privacy policy states this is the only case where tab data leaves your device.

2. Session Buddy, checked directly

Per its Chrome Web Store listing on September 24, 2026: version 4.1.2, Manifest V3, rated 4.7 out of 5 from about 25,100 ratings, 1,000,000+ users, completely free, no account required, local storage only.

Session Buddy saves open tabs and bookmarks into named collections, and lets you search across both open and saved tabs at once — a real advantage over a pure tab tool. Its automatic save isn't continuous, though: per the developer's own answers on the Session Buddy support forum, it saves a session automatically only when the browser is closed (by you, or by a crash), not on a rolling timer. And per a separate thread on the same forum, native Chrome tab groups (the colored groups) are not preserved on restore as of this writing, though the developer has described it as a planned feature.

4. Where OneTab and Session Buddy are better

OneTab is the fastest way to collapse a firehose of tabs into one click, has by far the largest install base of the three, and costs nothing. Session Buddy adds bookmark management and cross-search that neither OneTab nor Tab Lifeboat currently offer, has a strong 4.7★ rating over 25,000+ reviews, and is also completely free. Both have years of real-world use behind them; Tab Lifeboat does not yet.

FAQ

Does OneTab save tab groups or pinned tabs?

No. OneTab converts every open tab into a flat list; pinned status and native Chrome tab groups are not preserved on restore, confirmed by its own help documentation and independent reviews.

Does Session Buddy back up my tabs continuously?

Not continuously. Per the developer's own statements on Session Buddy's support forum, an automatic session is saved when the browser closes (including a crash), not on a running timer in the background.

Is OneTab or Session Buddy safe to use?

Both are long-established, widely used, free extensions on Manifest V3 with large review counts (14.6K and 25.1K respectively as of September 2026). OneTab's own privacy policy states tab data is never sent off-device except when you deliberately use its "share as a web page" feature.

Is Tab Lifeboat better than OneTab or Session Buddy?

Not universally — it's different. It preserves tab groups and pinned tabs and snapshots on a real schedule, which the other two don't fully do as of this writing, but it lacks Session Buddy's bookmark integration and OneTab's huge, battle-tested install base, and it isn't in the Chrome Web Store yet.
