Chrome closed all my tabs: how to get them back

After a crash, an update, a restart or one wrong click, try these in order, from quickest to last resort.

First, don't open and close Chrome over and over. Chrome keeps a record of your last sessions, and every restart can overwrite part of it. Work through the steps below calmly in the window you have.

1. Press Ctrl+Shift+T (Mac: Cmd+Shift+T)

This reopens the most recently closed tab or window. If you closed a whole window, one press brings back the whole window with all its tabs. Press it again to go further back. After a crash, Chrome usually shows a Restore button when it starts. If you still see it, click it first.

2. History → Recently closed

Open the Chrome menu (⋮) → History. The Recently closed list shows closed windows as "N tabs" entries. Click one to reopen that whole window. This also works when Ctrl+Shift+T has already moved past what you want.

3. Tabs from your other devices

If you're signed in to Chrome with sync on, open chrome://history/syncedTabs. It lists the open tabs of your other devices, which can include a laptop or phone where the same tabs are still open.

4. Search your history

If the window is gone from "Recently closed", the individual pages are still in your history (chrome://history, or Ctrl+H). Search for a word from the site or page title and reopen what you need. It's slower, but it finds almost everything you visited, unless you used an incognito window or cleared your history.

5. Last resort: the session files (advanced)

Chrome stores its last sessions in a Sessions folder inside your profile (for example %LOCALAPPDATA%\Google\Chrome\User Data\Default\Sessions on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/Google/Chrome/Default/Sessions on macOS, ~/.config/google-chrome/Default/Sessions on Linux). If nothing above worked, close Chrome and copy that folder somewhere safe before you try anything else. Tools and forum guides exist that read these files. Only try them on your copy, because Chrome overwrites the originals as it runs.

Make sure it doesn't happen again

  • Turn on "Continue where you left off": Settings → On startup → Continue where you left off. Chrome then reopens your tabs after a normal restart. It doesn't protect you from every crash or a window you closed yourself.
  • Save tab groups: recent Chrome versions let you save a tab group so it stays in the bookmarks bar after you close it.
  • Bookmark important sets: right-click a tab → Bookmark all tabs (or Ctrl+Shift+D) puts every open tab in a bookmarks folder.
